Marketplace Pricing Decomposition

A structured analytical framework for separating true rate changes from shifts in shipment mix, helping leaders understand why an average marketplace cost moved.

SQL, Python, BIPrimary tools
Rate-versus-mix decompositionMethod
Synthetic marketplace dataData

The challenge

An increase in average shipping cost does not automatically mean carrier rates increased. The result may instead be driven by a higher share of long-distance, heavy, expedited, or otherwise expensive shipments. Comparing averages alone can therefore lead to the wrong action.

Analytical approach

I divided transactions into comparable segments using carrier, service level, weight band, distance zone, and seller cohort. For each segment, the total change was separated into rate, mix, and interaction effects. 

Rate effect = (Current rate − Prior rate) × Prior mix
Mix effect  = (Current mix − Prior mix) × Prior rate
Interaction = (Current rate − Prior rate) × (Current mix − Prior mix)

Data preparation

  • Created a stable segment key across comparison periods.
  • Reconciled total volume and cost before decomposing the change.
  • Separated newly appearing and disappearing segments.
  • Applied minimum-volume thresholds to reduce noise.
  • Checked whether extreme outliers materially changed the conclusion.

Example executive output

DriverInterpretationPotential action
Rate effectPrice changed within comparable shipment segmentsReview contracts, discounts, or pricing rules
Mix effectVolume shifted toward more expensive segmentsInvestigate customer behavior and operating mix
InteractionRate and mix changed simultaneouslyReview the most influential combined segments

Business value

The framework prevents teams from treating every movement in an average as a pricing problem. It provides a traceable explanation of which segments drove the change and makes the final recommendation more defensible for finance, operations, and commercial stakeholders.

Limitations

  • Results depend on selecting dimensions that create genuinely comparable segments.
  • Very sparse segments can create unstable contributions.
  • The method explains movement but does not by itself prove causality.
